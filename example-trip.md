### What each stop adds

**Kokusai-dori — first impressions of central Naha**
“International Street” is Naha's main visitor-oriented shopping street, about 1.6 km long. Enjoy the colorful storefronts, snacks, souvenirs and street activity. Today it is an introduction and a route into the surrounding neighborhoods, with about 30–45 minutes of unhurried browsing.

**Makishi Public Market and surrounding lanes — food and exploration**
The market building contains fishmongers, butchers and other food sellers, with dining upstairs. One option is the *mochiage* system: buy eligible seafood or meat downstairs and have it cooked upstairs. Agree on ingredient and preparation costs before purchasing. Ordering a regular restaurant meal is equally suitable after the flight. Outside, explore the covered Heiwa-dori arcade and nearby lanes; Ukishima-dori offers additional cafés and independent shops. Allow roughly 1½–2 hours including lunch, rather than treating only the market building as the attraction.

**Tsuboya Yachimun Street — core afternoon stop**
*Yachimun* means pottery in Okinawan. This historic pottery district has a stone-paved street, ceramics shops, workshops and quieter lanes. Browse bowls, cups, plates and decorative pieces, and enjoy the neighborhood even if you do not buy anything. Plan 45–90 minutes including a café break. If available at your chosen café, *buku-buku cha*, a Ryukyuan tea topped with foam, is a local option to try. This stop is a priority for the walkable, charming neighborhood feel you prefer.

**Sakurazaka side streets — optional short detour**
An arts and nightlife neighborhood around the independent Sakurazaka Theater, with small streets, bars and an LGBTQ+-friendly atmosphere. A daytime visit is about the streetscape and any appealing small businesses; bars contribute more of the atmosphere later. Allow about 20–30 minutes and keep it flexible rather than treating it as a scheduled attraction visit.

**Easy Okinawan dinner — relaxed and close to the hotel**
Choose a casual Okinawan restaurant or izakaya without building the evening around a particular venue. A few shared dishes make a good introduction:

| Dish | What to expect |
|---|---|
| **Okinawa soba** | Wheat noodles in broth, commonly with pork; a comforting simple-meal option. |
| **Rafute** | Tender braised pork belly with rich, savory-sweet flavor. |
| **Goya champuru** | Bitter melon stir-fried with tofu, egg and commonly pork; the bitterness is characteristic. |
| **Jimami tofu** | Smooth, slightly springy peanut tofu; contains peanuts. |

**Energy / delay fallback:** Keep lunch and a shorter neighborhood walk, shorten Kokusai-dori browsing, and drop Sakurazaka first. Tsuboya is part of the preferred route, but can move to the existing October 31 alternative if you are too tired. Dinner can be a simple bowl of soba. ### Background and what to look for
- **Shuri:** Former royal capital of the Ryukyu Kingdom. The castle was its political and cultural center. Explore the gates, stone walls, views and interpretation at an unhurried pace. **The restored Seiden main hall interior opens November 23, 2026, after this trip**; do not book a post-reopening interior ticket for October 31. Recheck the accessible route shortly before travel.
- **Tamaudun:** Royal mausoleum built in 1501 for the second Sho dynasty; World Heritage site and National Treasure. A quieter complement to the castle, with limestone construction and guardian figures. Allow 30–45 minutes.
- **Kinjo stone-paved road:** A surviving roughly 300-meter section of a 16th-century route connecting Shuri to Naha Port. Stone walls, greenery and red-tiled buildings make it an atmospheric neighborhood walk. It ends downhill, not beside the monorail; allow onward transport.
- **Prefectural museum:** Planned afternoon stop for Okinawan history, culture and natural history. Prioritize the museum permanent exhibition; the art museum and special exhibitions are separate choices, not required additions. Allow about two hours. If tired, skip it without needing to reschedule the whole day.
- **Sakaemachi:** Older neighborhood market arcade whose small bars and eateries become the focus in the evening. Wander briefly, then settle into an inviting izakaya; a bar crawl is not required.

# Appendix — Getting Around Taipei (restored and expanded)
Walk within neighborhoods; MRT between districts; taxi for luggage, fatigue or awkward transfers. Hotel base: **Zhongshan R11/G14**, Red and Green lines.
- One EasyCard per person; add value and carry cash for top-ups. Tap in/out on MRT, and on/off city buses. Ordinary Taipei MRT fares generally **NT$20–65** by distance; verify at the machine. Airport MRT is separate **NT$160 A1–A12** each way. No seat reservation needed for normal MRT travel.
- **Red Line:** Zhongshan → Taipei Main, Chiang Kai-shek Memorial Hall and Taipei 101/World Trade Center southbound; Shilin/Beitou northbound. For Xinbeitou change at Beitou to the branch.
- **Green Line:** Zhongshan → Beimen/Ximen toward Xindian; Songshan/Raohe toward Songshan.
- **Blue Line:** Transfer at Taipei Main or Ximen for Longshan Temple, or travel east for Zhongxiao Xinsheng and Taipei City Hall.
- **Orange Line:** Useful for other districts including Xingtian Temple; route as needed.
- National Palace Museum requires a bus/taxi leg; it is not directly on the MRT. Follow real-time stop/direction information if using a bus.
- Airport **A1** platforms and Taipei Metro **Taipei Main** platforms are different: allow walking/wayfinding time. With bags, taxi from A1 to Royal-Nikko avoids that interchange. Express train time **A12–A1 ~35 minutes**, not door-to-door.
- Taxis: show the Chinese hotel name/address; arrange with reception when helpful. Taxi fares vary and are not included in admission budgets.
- YouBike remains optional; verify visitor registration/payment before relying on it. EasyCard alone does not complete registration.
